# Error Taxonomy

Assign one to three labels per wrong or partial-credit item. Prefer specific labels over vague comments.

## Core Labels

- calculation habit: arithmetic, copying, vertical calculation, decimal/fraction, or simplification error
- sign error: positive/negative, inequality direction, coordinate sign, or vector direction error
- concept unclear: definition, theorem meaning, or object type misunderstood
- formula misuse: formula remembered incorrectly or used outside its conditions
- condition missed: ignored range, hidden constraint, unit, domain, or problem requirement
- reading error: misread the question, asked quantity, diagram label, or data
- method choice: chose a cumbersome or invalid method
- algebra transformation: invalid equation transformation, factorization, expansion, substitution, or cancellation
- process gap: missing key steps, unsupported conclusion, or answer-only solution when reasoning is required
- proof rigor: circular reasoning, unproved claim, incomplete logical chain, or visual assumption
- diagram reasoning: geometry/graph/solid relation inferred only from the drawing
- notation issue: ambiguous symbols, missing constants, wrong interval/set notation, or mismatched variables
- time management: blank, rushed, or unfinished despite accessible method

## Level Notes

Elementary:

- Prefer labels such as calculation habit, reading error, condition missed, and unit issue.
- Translate labels into friendly language in the final report.

Middle and high school:

- Use topic-specific labels when helpful: function image, parameter classification, auxiliary line, trigonometric identity, sequence recursion, or analytic-geometry setup.

University:

- Use rigor labels when theorem conditions, quantifiers, domain/codomain, matrix dimensions, convergence assumptions, or probability independence are mishandled.

## Priority

Use:

- High: repeated error, foundational topic, large score loss, or blocks future learning.
- Medium: important but localized error.
- Low: isolated slip with clear understanding.
